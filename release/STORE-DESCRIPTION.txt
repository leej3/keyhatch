Keyhatch gives local tools access to credentials you deliberately choose to share.

Import one password or token, preserve its original expiry, and keep it encrypted on your Mac. Unlock with Touch ID, then approve the bundled command-line client to retrieve that credential while your session and approval remain valid.

• Selective import: no bulk access to Apple Passwords or another password store.
• Local encrypted storage with Secure Enclave protection for key use.
• Sessions lasting at most five hours and credential availability bounded by the original expiry, at most three days from retrieval.
• Explicit approval, revocation, lock, deletion, and recent encrypted activity history.
• A host-scoped Git credential helper and command-line status and lock controls.
• No developer-operated cloud vault, analytics, advertising, or subscription service.

The Mac App Store edition uses separate sandboxes for the app and its bundled client. It does not run arbitrary commands or install a background service. The client remains inside the app bundle; setup instructions explain how to invoke it from Terminal.

Credential handoff is intentional: any tool that receives a secret can retain it. Other tools under your macOS account may invoke the approved client. Locking or revoking access stops future retrieval but cannot retract a disclosed secret or end an existing remote session.

Requires macOS 14 or later, a Mac with Secure Enclave, and configured Touch ID. Keyhatch has no password fallback for its credential unlock. Your original password store remains separate and should remain your recovery source.
